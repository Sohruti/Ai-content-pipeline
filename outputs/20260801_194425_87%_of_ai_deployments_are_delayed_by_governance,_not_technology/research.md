## Topic: 87% of AI Deployments Are Delayed by Governance, Not Technology

## Industry Trends:
- The majority of AI deployments are hindered by governance issues rather than technological limitations.
- There is a significant gap between AI investment and the practical steps needed to put systems into day-to-day use, with security reviews and compliance demands being major obstacles.
- Enterprises are making trade-offs by deploying AI with reduced capabilities rather than solving the underlying governance problem, leading to a widening gap between AI investment and business value.

## AI News:
- Recent studies and surveys indicate that 87% of companies have delayed AI deployments due to data governance issues, and 82.9% of organizations have seen AI projects delayed because of security or compliance reviews.
- The EU AI Act and other regulatory efforts are creating compliance confusion, with potential fines for non-compliance, further complicating AI adoption.
- Despite the challenges, AI is expected to unlock significant productivity boosts, with McKinsey estimating a $4.4 trillion potential increase.

## Competitor Insights:
- AvePoint highlights the importance of data governance in successful AI adoption, noting that AI-generated content is increasingly becoming a significant portion of company data.
- Enterprise Management Associates emphasizes that security, governance, and compliance are now the primary barriers to moving AI into production, with many organizations compensating by deploying AI with reduced capabilities.
- Companies like Second Order Systems (2OS) are focusing on building AI infrastructure that prioritizes privacy and security, recognizing the need for enterprise-grade solutions that do not compromise on data sovereignty.

## Market Context:
- The failure rate of AI projects is high, with 95% of projects failing to reach production, often due to governance and compliance gaps, inadequate bias detection, and reputational and legal risks.
- Bridging the gap between AI ambition and execution requires addressing the human element, including skills gaps, talent recruitment, and adapting AI models to regional contexts and languages.
- The Middle East, in particular, faces additional challenges such as adapting Western-built AI models to Arabic language processing and local cultural contexts, as well as integrating with legacy systems.

## Raw Research:
- Key findings include the significant delay in AI deployments due to governance issues, the importance of data governance, and the need for AI infrastructure that prioritizes privacy and security.
- Data points such as the 87% delay rate, 82.9% of organizations experiencing delays due to security or compliance reviews, and the estimated $4.4 trillion productivity boost potential of AI underscore the business implications of effective AI governance and deployment.
- The average time for AI projects to move from prototype to deployment is 8 months, with only 48% of projects globally making it to production, highlighting the need for streamlined governance and compliance processes.

In conclusion, the research highlights the critical role of governance in AI deployments, with the majority of delays and failures attributed to governance issues rather than technological limitations. Addressing these governance challenges is essential for unlocking the potential of AI in enterprises, requiring a focus on data governance, compliance, security, and the development of AI infrastructure that prioritizes privacy and security.

**Enterprise AI deployments slowed by security reviews**
The report, produced by Enterprise Management Associates and based on a survey of IT and security leaders, found that 82.9% of organisations had seen AI projects delayed before reaching production because of security or compliance reviews. It also found that 81.6% had deployed AI in a reduced form because of security concerns, including limits on agent autonomy or data access.

The findings point to a gap between AI investment and the practical steps needed to put systems into day-to-day use. More than two-thirds of delayed projects remained stalled for at least a month, while nearly 30% were delayed for four months or longer. [...] Enterprise Management Associates said the survey showed companies are often making trade-offs rather than solving the underlying governance problem. "Security, governance and compliance are now the primary barriers preventing enterprises from moving AI into production at the pace the business demands," said Chris Steffen, Vice President of Research, Information Security at EMA. "The findings make clear that many organizations are compensating by deploying AI with reduced capabilities rather than the functionality originally intended. That compromise is widening the gap between AI investment and business value, while adding avoidable costs, delays and operational inefficiencies."

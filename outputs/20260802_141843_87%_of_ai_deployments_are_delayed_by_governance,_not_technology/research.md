## Topic: 87% of AI Deployments Are Delayed by Governance, Not Technology

## Industry Trends:
- The majority of organizations are experiencing delays in AI deployments, primarily due to governance issues rather than technological limitations.
- Data security and compliance are significant concerns, with many organizations struggling to balance the need for AI adoption with the requirement for robust governance.
- The trend indicates a shift towards recognizing the importance of governance in AI deployment, with organizations beginning to understand that effective governance is crucial for successful AI integration.
- There is a growing operational trust gap between organizations' AI ambitions and their confidence in the guardrails meant to support them.

## AI News:
- Recent research from AvePoint highlights that 86% of organizations delayed AI deployments by up to a year due to security and data quality concerns.
- The average AI deployment is delayed by nearly six months, with one in four organizations experiencing postponements of up to 12 months.
- Inaccurate AI outputs and concerns about data security are leading reasons for delaying generative AI assistant rollouts.
- AI hallucinations, where AI systems produce plausible but incorrect information, are identified as a significant threat by nearly a third of organizations.

## Competitor Insights:
- Competitors are focusing on building governance directly into their AI workflows rather than treating it as an afterthought.
- Organizations that are succeeding in AI adoption are those that prioritize governance and security, recognizing that these aspects are fundamental to successful AI integration.
- There is a notable gap between AI specialists who build models and the general workforce who could implement them, indicating a need for AI fluency across all roles.

## Market Context:
- The market is experiencing an exponential growth in AI-generated content, fundamentally changing how organizations must approach data security and governance.
- Enterprises are struggling not just with the volume of new data but with maintaining data lineage and ensuring quality control when AI systems are both consuming and creating information at scale.
- The EU AI Act’s stringent requirements and potential fines are creating compliance confusion and reputational risks for organizations.
- The projected AI governance market is expected to grow significantly, from ~$340M to $4.83B by 2034, indicating a growing recognition of the importance of governance in AI.

This is the blind spot many organizations are now confronting: Governance failures do not always appear as visible incidents. Sometimes they appear as organizational hesitation, and as AI systems become more autonomous, hesitation carries its own risks. [...] The challenge is no longer whether organizations should prioritize security or speed, but how to advance both at once. When the balance between protecting data and enabling adoption tips too far toward caution, teams stall due to governance gridlock. And when that delay persists, the consequences extend far beyond delayed AI adoption timelines.

## AI-Ready Governance Was Designed for a Different Operating Model

Most organizations already have some form of AI guidelines in place: responsible AI principles, governance committees, and acceptable use policies. The problem is that many of those guardrails were built for systems operating at human speed. [...] This is how shadow AI spreads. Shadow AI – the use of unsanctioned AI tools outside formal governance processes – is often less a result of recklessness and more a symptom of governance models struggling to support AI adoption at operational speed. Teams continue experimenting independently, sensitive information moves into unapproved systems, and AI usage expands beyond centralized oversight.

The longer governance gridlock persists, the more fragmented AI adoption becomes. Governance concerns slow deployment, delayed deployment drives shadow AI usage, and fragmented AI adoption further increases operational risk.
